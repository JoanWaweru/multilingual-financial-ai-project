SACCO vs Money Market Fund (MMF) — Kenya

This note provides general, non-numeric guidance. It is not a recommendation of any specific SACCO or fund.

SACCOs are member-owned cooperatives. Members save regularly and can access loans under cooperative rules. SACCOs typically require membership, may have share capital requirements, and offer dividends or interest based on performance. SACCOs are regulated by SASRA for deposit-taking SACCOs.

Money Market Funds (MMFs) are collective investment schemes regulated by the Capital Markets Authority (CMA). An MMF invests in short-term, low-risk instruments such as Treasury Bills, bank deposits, and commercial paper. MMFs are designed for liquidity and capital preservation, and returns vary with market conditions.

Key differences:
- Ownership: SACCOs are member-owned; MMFs are pooled investment funds.
- Access to loans: SACCOs typically provide loans to members; MMFs do not.
- Liquidity: MMFs generally allow quicker withdrawals; SACCO rules vary by cooperative.
- Regulation: SACCOs fall under SASRA (deposit-taking); MMFs fall under CMA.

Always confirm current terms, fees, and rules directly with the SACCO or fund provider.
